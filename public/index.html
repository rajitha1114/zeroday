10 December 2026. New Australian Privacy Principle 1.7 commences. If a computer program uses personal information to make — or substantially help make — a decision that significantly affects someone, your privacy policy has to say so, and say what information and what decisions. It is a disclosure duty, not a ban. Most companies will discover they are in scope and have nothing written.

The deadline nobody has budgeted for

APP 1.7 commences 10 December 2026. Most Australian companies are in scope and don't know it.

You do not need a machine-learning model to be caught by this. A rules engine that declines an application, a scoring tool that ranks job applicants, a system that decides who gets a payment plan — if personal information goes in and a decision that significantly affects someone comes out, the disclosure duty applies. It reaches decisions your vendors make on your behalf, too, which is where most companies will be caught short.

  1. Find every in-scope system, including the ones you boughtYour own code, your SaaS platforms, and anything a supplier runs for you. Procurement contracts rarely say. Someone has to ask.
  2. Decide what is actually in scope, and write down whyThe judgement calls are around “substantially and directly related to making a decision” and “significantly affect”. Getting them right matters less than having a defensible, documented reason for each call.
  3. Draft the privacy policy disclosureThe three things APP 1.8 requires: the kinds of personal information used, the kinds of decisions made solely by the program, and the kinds it substantially helps make. In plain English, because that is the point of the rule.
  4. Leave behind a register you can maintainA forward-looking duty, not a one-off. Every new AI tool changes the answer, so it needs somewhere to live and someone who updates it.

Don't take our word for it. Go and count.

Six read-only numbers you can pull from your own tenant in about fifteen minutes. Put them in below and the arithmetic is done in front of you — every figure traceable to a number you supplied. If it comes back fine, you've spent fifteen minutes and learned something useful. That is a perfectly good outcome for us.

01How many SharePoint sites do you have?

SharePoint admin centre → Sites → Active sites. The total at the top of the list. Include Teams-connected sites; every one of them is a document library.

Connect-SPOService -Url https://YOURTENANT-admin.sharepoint.com (Get-SPOSite -Limit All).Count
02How many have no sensitivity label?

Same list, add the Sensitivity column and sort. Unlabelled means nothing downstream — not DLP, not Restricted Content Discovery, not conditional access — can treat that site differently from the staff canteen menu.

(Get-SPOSite -Limit All | Where-Object { -not $_.SensitivityLabel }).Count
03How many haven't been touched in six months?

Sort Active sites by Last activity. These are the dangerous ones: the project that ended, the acquisition that closed, the team that dissolved. Nobody owns them, nobody reviews them, and an assistant reads them exactly as eagerly as it reads today's work.

(Get-SPOSite -Limit All | Where-Object { $_.LastContentModifiedDate -lt (Get-Date).AddDays(-180) }).Count
04How many live “Anyone” links are out there?

Microsoft 365 admin centre → Reports → Usage → SharePoint → Sharing, or the Purview activity explorer filtered to anonymous link creation. These are reachable with no login at all. If the number isn't zero, deal with it before you deal with anything else on this page.

05How many licensed users?

Microsoft 365 admin centre → Billing → Licences. Use the number of people who can actually sign in and ask a question.

06Roughly how many files per site?

SharePoint admin centre → Sites shows storage used per site; a reasonable working figure for most tenants is 800–2,000. If you have no idea, leave the default. The output scales linearly with this, so it's the one number worth checking rather than guessing.

The first certifiable AI management standard, adopted here as AS ISO/IEC 42001. What turns “we're careful with AI” into something a customer or a board can be shown. Certifications are still uncommon and accredited auditors are scarce, which is exactly why being early is worth something in a tender.

What international and European customers recognise. Full ISMS build: scope, risk methodology, Statement of Applicability, internal audit and management review.

SOC 2 Type 1 & Type 2

What North American and enterprise buyers ask for by name. Trust Services Criteria scoping, control design, and evidence that survives an observation window.

Engineering time

A first certification typically absorbs a third to a half of a senior engineer for four to six months, plus a manager coordinating it. That's a feature you didn't ship, paid for at your most expensive internal rate.

02

Learning on the clock

Nobody internally has done it before, so the first attempt is also the training run. Scoping errors and misread controls surface during fieldwork, which is the most expensive possible moment to find them.

03

Key-person risk

The knowledge ends up in one person's head. When they take leave — or another job — the evidence trail and the auditor relationship go with them.

04

It comes back every year

ISO 27001 brings annual surveillance audits and recertification every three years; SOC 2 needs a fresh report each year. Access reviews, register upkeep and incident exercises run on a cycle, indefinitely.

Before you ask

The questions that come up on every first call.

Why would we give a stranger read access to everything we own?

You shouldn't, and the engagement is designed so you don't have to trust us to start. Access is read-only, scoped to the directories and services in the statement of work, granted through your own conditional access, time-boxed to the ten days, and revoked on delivery. You can watch every call we make in your own audit log — we'll tell you which log and how to read it on day one.

We carry professional indemnity and cyber liability cover, we'll sign your NDA rather than ours, and the exposure register lives in your tenant, not ours. If you'd rather we never touch the environment, there is a version where your admin runs our scripts and hands us the output. It's slower and slightly less accurate, and about a third of clients choose it.

Can't you just give us software we install, like Microsoft does?

Not honestly, no — and anyone who says otherwise is selling you a dashboard. The controls that stop an assistant surfacing the wrong thing live inside the platform you already own. In Microsoft 365 that's Purview labelling, Restricted Access Control and sharing policy. In Google Workspace it's Drive labels, DLP and shared-drive membership. Different levers, different order, different failure modes.

We already turned Copilot on. Is it too late?

No, and it's the more common situation. Nothing has necessarily gone wrong — but you now have a live retrieval surface over your entire content estate and usually no record of what has been asked or returned.

The assessment is the same with two additions: we read the interaction logs you already hold to see what has actually been retrieved, and we sequence remediation so the highest-exposure content closes first rather than doing a tidy alphabetical sweep.

Are we really in scope for APP 1.7? We don't use AI for decisions.

Possibly not — and finding that out and documenting the reasoning is itself the deliverable. But the rule is written around “a computer program”, not around AI, and it captures decisions a program substantially and directly helps make, not only ones it makes alone. Credit or eligibility scoring, application triage, rostering that affects pay, automated account suspension, fraud flags that block a transaction, applicant ranking — none of these need a model to be caught.

The part that catches most companies is that it reaches arrangements where a supplier's program makes the decision for you. Your privacy policy has to describe that too, and your contracts usually don't give you the visibility to describe it accurately.

We already bought a compliance platform. Do we still need you?

Vanta, Drata and Sprinto are good at watching controls you've already built and collecting evidence from them automatically. They aren't good at deciding what your controls should be, writing policies that match how your company actually works, or configuring the underlying systems so the checks go green for real reasons.

Most teams who stall have a platform showing 60% and no idea which of the remaining 40% is a five-minute fix and which is three months of work. That gap is the job.

Can you work with the auditor we've already chosen?

Yes, and it's the normal arrangement. We're not auditors and don't issue reports, so there's no independence question — we sit on your side and manage the relationship, the request list and the findings.

Can you do an IRAP assessment?

No. IRAP assessments must be performed by an ASD-endorsed assessor and we don't hold that endorsement. Anyone telling you otherwise is worth a second look.

We can get you ready for one, and help you close what the assessor finds.
