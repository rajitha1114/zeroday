—days to APP 1.7

10 December 2026. New Australian Privacy Principle 1.7 commences. If a computer program uses personal information to make — or substantially help make — a decision that significantly affects someone, your privacy policy has to say so, and say what information and what decisions. It is a disclosure duty, not a ban. Most companies will discover they are in scope and have nothing written.

The deadline nobody has budgeted for

APP 1.7 commences 10 December 2026. Most Australian companies are in scope and don't know it.

You don't need a machine-learning model to be caught by this. A rules engine that declines an application, a tool that ranks job applicants, a system that decides who gets a payment plan. It reaches decisions your vendors make for you, too — which is where most companies get caught short.

  1. Find every in-scope system, including the ones you boughtYour own code, your SaaS platforms, and anything a supplier runs for you. Procurement contracts rarely say. Someone has to ask.
  2. Decide what is actually in scope, and write down whyThe judgement calls sit around “substantially and directly related” and “significantly affect”. Getting them right matters less than having a documented reason for each.
  3. Draft the privacy policy disclosureThe three things APP 1.8 requires: the kinds of personal information used, the kinds of decisions made solely by the program, and the kinds it substantially helps make. In plain English, because that is the point of the rule.
  4. Leave behind a register you can maintainA forward-looking duty, not a one-off. Every new AI tool changes the answer, so it needs somewhere to live and someone who updates it.

Don't take our word for it. Go and count.

Six read-only numbers from your own tenant, fifteen minutes. The arithmetic happens in front of you, every figure traceable to something you supplied. If it comes back fine, that's a perfectly good outcome for us.

01How many SharePoint sites do you have?

SharePoint admin centre → Sites → Active sites. Include Teams-connected sites — every one is a document library.

Connect-SPOService -Url https://YOURTENANT-admin.sharepoint.com (Get-SPOSite -Limit All).Count
02How many have no sensitivity label?

Same list, add the Sensitivity column. Unlabelled means nothing downstream — not DLP, not Restricted Content Discovery — can tell that site apart from the canteen menu.

(Get-SPOSite -Limit All | Where-Object { -not $_.SensitivityLabel }).Count
03How many haven't been touched in six months?

Sort by Last activity. The project that ended, the acquisition that closed, the team that dissolved. Nobody owns them, and an assistant reads them as eagerly as today's work.

(Get-SPOSite -Limit All | Where-Object { $_.LastContentModifiedDate -lt (Get-Date).AddDays(-180) }).Count
04How many live “Anyone” links are out there?

Microsoft 365 admin centre → Reports → Usage → SharePoint → Sharing. Reachable with no login at all. If this isn't zero, deal with it before anything else on this page.

05How many licensed users?

Microsoft 365 admin centre → Billing → Licences. Everyone who can sign in and ask a question.

06Roughly how many files per site?

Most tenants land between 800 and 2,000. The output scales linearly with this, so it's the one worth checking rather than guessing.

What these six numbers cannot tell you: which documents are exposed, who specifically can reach them, or how the access was granted — inherited from a parent site, an open link, an orphaned group, or somebody clicking share in 2019. That last distinction is the whole of the remediation plan, because each one is fixed differently and takes a different amount of time. Counting sites is a thermometer. The assessment is the diagnosis.

Certifications, when you need them

The same control set carries most of the way into the next standard.

Most companies end up needing more than one. Assessed together, the overlap does the work — and the AI governance evidence sits on top of the same foundation rather than beside it.

ISO/IEC 42001

The first certifiable AI management standard, adopted here as AS ISO/IEC 42001. Turns “we're careful with AI” into something a board can be shown. Certifications are still rare and auditors scarce, which is why being early is worth something in a tender.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022

What international and European customers recognise. Full ISMS build: scope, risk methodology, Statement of Applicability, internal audit and management review.

SOC 2 Type 1 & Type 2

What North American and enterprise buyers ask for by name. Trust Services Criteria scoping, control design, and evidence that survives an observation window.

Essential Eight & the ASD guidance

Still what insurers, tenders and assessors measure against. We assess against the current maturity model and build uplift that carries forward rather than becoming work you redo.

APRA CPS 234 & CPS 230

If you supply banks, insurers or super funds, these obligations reach you through your contracts. Information security capability, incident notification, and operational risk for material service providers.

Privacy Act & NZ Privacy Act 2020

APP 1.7 disclosure, the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, and the statutory tort of serious invasion of privacy live since June 2025. Plus NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act if you sell into Europe.

Before you ask

The questions that come up on every first call.

Why would we give a stranger read access to everything we own?

You shouldn't have to trust us to start. Access is read-only, scoped in the statement of work, granted through your own conditional access, time-boxed, and revoked on delivery — and every call we make appears in your audit log. We sign your NDA, not ours, and the register lives in your tenant.

If you'd rather we never touch the environment, your admin can run our scripts and hand us the output. About a third of clients choose that.

Can't you just give us software we install, like Microsoft does?

Not honestly — anyone who says otherwise is selling you a dashboard. The controls live inside the platform you already own: Purview labelling and Restricted Access Control in Microsoft 365, Drive labels and DLP in Google Workspace. Different levers, different failure modes. What we make repeatable is the assessment.

We already turned Copilot on. Is it too late?

No — it's the more common situation. You now have a live retrieval surface over your whole estate and usually no record of what's been asked. The assessment adds two things: we read the interaction logs you already hold, and we close the highest-exposure content first.

Are we really in scope for APP 1.7? We don't use AI for decisions.

Possibly not — and documenting that reasoning is itself the deliverable. But the rule says “computer program”, not AI, and covers decisions a program substantially helps make. Credit scoring, application triage, rostering that affects pay, fraud flags, applicant ranking: none need a model.

What catches most companies is that it reaches decisions a supplier's program makes for you — and your contracts rarely give you the visibility to describe those accurately.

We already bought a compliance platform. Do we still need you?

Vanta, Drata and Sprinto watch controls you've already built. They don't decide what those controls should be, write policies that match your business, or configure the systems so the checks go green for real reasons. Most teams who stall sit at 60% with no idea which of the remaining 40% is an afternoon and which is three months. That gap is the job.

Can you work with the auditor we've already chosen?

Yes — the normal arrangement. We're not auditors and don't issue reports, so there's no independence question. We sit on your side and manage the relationship, the request list and the findings. If you haven't picked one, we'll shortlist firms that suit your size rather than price for enterprise.

Can you do an IRAP assessment?

No. IRAP assessments must be performed by an ASD-endorsed assessor and we don't hold that endorsement — anyone telling you otherwise is worth a second look. We can get you ready for one and help you close what the assessor finds.
